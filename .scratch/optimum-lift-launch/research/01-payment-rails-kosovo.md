# Payment Rails for a Kosovo-Based Digital-Goods Merchant

Research date: 2026-09-01. Scope: a Kosovo-based individual selling downloadable PDF fitness/diet plans via WooCommerce on WordPress, to EU/US buyers, driven by cold Meta ads traffic. Covers both a registered Kosovo business and a merchant with no registered entity at all.

## Bottom line

**Kosovo is a genuinely hard case.** It is unsupported, absent, or ambiguous on almost every major global payment platform's country list — Stripe, PayPal, Mollie, Adyen, Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy and Razorpay all explicitly or effectively exclude it. The two things that actually check out as real, testable options this week are below, ranked.

1. **Stripe via Stripe Atlas (form a US Delaware company), then use standard Stripe + Stripe's official WooCommerce plugin.** This is the closest thing to a "just works for cold-traffic card impulse buys" answer, because it puts you on Stripe's actual card-acceptance stack (3D Secure/SCA, Apple Pay/Google Pay, Link, fraud tooling) with a first-party WooCommerce integration. **Biggest catch:** it costs $500 upfront + $100/year ([Stripe's own pricing](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup)), requires running a small US entity (Delaware franchise tax, registered agent, likely IRS Form 5472/1120 filings for a foreign-owned US company — outside this research's scope but a real ongoing obligation), Stripe explicitly will not guarantee your business gets approved to actually process payments once formed, and it does **nothing** for EU VAT — you (now as a US LLC/C-corp) still have to self-register for the non-Union OSS scheme and file/remit EU VAT yourself, because Stripe is a payment processor, not a merchant/seller of record ([Stripe's own explanation](https://stripe.com/resources/more/seller-of-record)).

2. **A Kosovo bank's own e-commerce card-acquiring gateway** — Raiffeisen Bank Kosovo is the strongest pick because it has an *officially bank-maintained* WooCommerce plugin on WordPress.org ([Raiffeisen Payment Gateway](https://wordpress.org/plugins/raiffeisen-payment-gateway/), built by RaiAccept, referenced from [Raiffeisen Kosovo's own e-commerce page](https://www.raiffeisen-kosovo.com/en/business/pranimi-i-pagesave/raiffeisen-e-commerce.html)); ProCredit Bank Kosovo is a close second with its own WooCommerce plugin offering ([ProCredit's own e-commerce page](https://www.procreditbank-kos.com/eng/for-your-business/digital-services/e-commerce/)). This gets you a real, native WooCommerce checkout with Visa/Mastercard acceptance from any country, no foreign entity needed. **Biggest catch:** every Kosovo bank that publishes eligibility criteria requires you to be a **registered Kosovo business** — ProCredit says explicitly it's for "legal entities and entrepreneurs registered and operate as businesses in the Republic of Kosovo" — so a true zero-registration individual cannot use this path until they register at least as a "Biznes Individual" (Kosovo's cheap, fast sole-trader registration). It also leaves 100% of EU VAT/OSS compliance on you, and there is **no independent evidence found** (positive or negative) about how well these gateways actually convert cold, cross-border Meta-ads card traffic — that risk is untested, not just unconfirmed on paper.

3. **Paddle, as a VAT-handling fallback — but do not assume it plugs into WooCommerce.** Paddle is the one Merchant of Record whose *unsupported*-country list does not name Kosovo ([Paddle's own supported-countries page](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/which-countries-are-supported-by-paddle)), so it is worth an actual signup attempt to see if Kosovo is accepted at KYC — but Paddle does not confirm Kosovo is supported either, and it would absorb EU VAT for you if it does accept you. **Biggest catch, and the one not to bury:** Paddle's own help center says plainly, **"Paddle no longer supports 3rd party online stores directly"** and that any WordPress/WooCommerce plugin is unofficial — Paddle explicitly disclaims responsibility and technical support for those plugins ([Paddle's own FAQ on WordPress](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/does-paddle-integrate-with-wordpress), [Paddle's own FAQ on 3rd-party stores](https://paddle.com/support/does-paddle-support-3rd-party-online-stores-eg-woocommerce-magento-shopify)).

**Critical finding that could change the platform decision:** every Merchant-of-Record option investigated (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Gumroad, FastSpring) either has **no official WooCommerce integration at all**, or an official plugin that explicitly does **not** connect to WooCommerce, or an official WordPress plugin the provider itself says is **no longer maintained** (see §6 for the full breakdown). If a Kosovo seller ends up needing an MoR because Stripe Atlas and the local-bank routes both fall through, the realistic result is checkout happening on the MoR's own hosted page (redirect/overlay), with WooCommerce reduced to a product catalog/landing page. That is a materially different, extra-hop checkout experience for cold, impulse-buy Meta traffic than a native in-page WooCommerce checkout — worth weighing against a purpose-built hosted store (e.g., a simple landing page + the MoR's own checkout, or a platform with native MoR support) **before** committing to "WooCommerce on WordPress" as the platform.

**On the individual-vs-registered-business question specifically:** Stripe Atlas is indifferent to Kosovo registration status (it forms a brand-new US entity regardless). The Kosovo-bank route requires at least a registered "Biznes Individual." Paddle/MoRs generally accept individual creators without a company, which is the main advantage of pursuing the MoR path in parallel if the merchant genuinely has zero registration and wants to test-sell before committing to a Kosovo business registration or a $500 Stripe Atlas company.

## 1. Stripe

Kosovo is **not** a supported country for a Stripe merchant/business account. Stripe's own global-availability page lists 50+ supported countries/regions alphabetically, and Kosovo does not appear on it (Serbia does not appear either) — confirmed by direct inspection of [stripe.com/global](https://stripe.com/global).

**Workaround: Stripe Atlas.** Stripe's own documentation confirms Atlas is designed exactly for this kind of gap: *"Stripe Atlas incorporates your startup and gives you everything you need to open a bank account, accept payments, and fundraise in two business days"* ([docs.stripe.com/atlas](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas)). Concretely, per Stripe's own signup docs ([docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup)):
- Cost: **"Atlas costs 500 USD, which covers incorporation (plus state fees), and your first year of registered agent services. After that, we charge 100 USD annually to maintain your registered agent."**
- It forms a Delaware LLC or C-Corp, gets an EIN from the IRS, and — notably — does **not** require the founder to have a US Social Security Number: *"SSN or ITIN"* is accepted, so a Kosovo founder can use an ITIN instead (EIN processing simply takes longer: "If the founder doesn't have an SSN, Atlas will get their company's EIN in 10–50 business days after incorporation").
- Founders need a company address, a US phone number (a virtual one via an Atlas partner is explicitly offered), and to submit personal ID/DOB info.
- Once incorporated, Stripe explicitly says *"you can start accepting US card payments on Stripe... before receiving your EIN"* — but also caveats: **"Atlas can't guarantee that your business will be approved to use Stripe payments."**
- Atlas does not provide legal/tax/accounting advice and explicitly is not a substitute for advice from a lawyer/accountant — ongoing Delaware franchise-tax filings and registered-agent renewal are the buyer's responsibility.

This gives access to Stripe's native, well-documented, officially co-maintained WooCommerce plugin (see §6), but it does not change who is responsible for EU VAT — see §5.

## 2. PayPal

PayPal does **not** currently let a Kosovo resident/business open an account to send or receive money as a standard user. Two lines of official-source evidence:

- PayPal's live, JavaScript-rendered "PayPal Global | List of Countries and Currencies" page (e.g. [paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/country-worldwide](https://www.paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/country-worldwide)) could not be rendered by this research's fetch tooling (it loads its country list dynamically) — **this is disclosed rather than guessed around.** However, PayPal's own developer documentation archive explicitly and separately names Kosovo (not merged into Serbia) as one of a fixed list of markets excluded from certain PayPal services "due to legal constraints": *"Mobile Express Checkout is available almost everywhere PayPal is accepted, with certain exceptions due to legal constraints. These are Albania, Andorra, Armenia, Belarus, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, Georgia, India, Kosovo, Macedonia, Moldavia, Monaco, Montenegro, Russia, Serbia, Ukraine, and Vatican City State"* ([developer.paypal.com archive docs](https://developer.paypal.com/docs/archive/express-checkout/mobile-express-checkout/)). This at minimum confirms PayPal's own systems treat Kosovo as a distinct, separately-flagged, restricted jurisdiction, not silently grouped under Serbia.
- PayPal's own developer country-codes reference lists Serbia (code `RS`) but has **no entry for Kosovo at all** ([developer.paypal.com/api/rest/reference/country-codes/](https://developer.paypal.com/api/rest/reference/country-codes/)).
- Corroborating (non-primary, used only as a lead, not as the basis of the claim) — PayPal's own community forum has multiple long-running threads from Kosovo residents confirming they cannot register PayPal accounts, and change.org petitions addressed to PayPal asking for Kosovo to be added, none resolved as of this research (e.g. [PayPal Community thread](https://www.paypal-community.com/t5/Reporting/Why-is-paypal-not-available-in-Kosovo/td-p/3171207)).

**Net: PayPal is not usable by a Kosovo-based seller to receive payments, for either a registered business or an individual.** There is no PayPal-published workaround analogous to Stripe Atlas.

### Kosovo-local / Balkan-regional bank acquiring

This is the most concrete positive finding of this research: **every major Kosovo bank checked offers its own online card-acquiring / e-commerce gateway, and two of them explicitly offer WooCommerce integration.**

- **ProCredit Bank Kosovo** — has a dedicated e-Commerce service. Own-site quote: *"legal entities and entrepreneurs that are registered and operate as businesses in the Republic of Kosovo and that have a website or a mobile application"* are eligible; supports Visa/Mastercard; explicitly states *"ready-made plugins for OpenCart, Joomla, Drupal, C#, JavaScript and WooCommerce"*; recently upgraded to an EMV 3DS 2.2.0-compliant gateway. Whether this WooCommerce plugin is separately published/listed on WordPress.org (vs. distributed only to onboarded merchants) was **not confirmed**. [ProCredit Bank Kosovo's own e-Commerce page](https://www.procreditbank-kos.com/eng/for-your-business/digital-services/e-commerce/)
- **Raiffeisen Bank Kosovo** — has an e-Commerce product with an accompanying **officially bank-maintained** WooCommerce plugin, described on Raiffeisen's own site as *"officially available on the WordPress Marketplace"*: *"Easily connect your online store with Raiffeisen's secure payment solution through the new Raiffeisen Payment Gateway Plugin."* Accepts Visa/Mastercard (debit and credit). [Raiffeisen Kosovo's own e-Commerce page](https://www.raiffeisen-kosovo.com/en/business/pranimi-i-pagesave/raiffeisen-e-commerce.html); plugin listing at [wordpress.org/plugins/raiffeisen-payment-gateway](https://wordpress.org/plugins/raiffeisen-payment-gateway/) (maintained by RaiAccept, Raiffeisen's payment-processing arm; supports WooCommerce order management, refunds, and one-click checkout).
- **TEB Kosovo (TEB SHA)** — has an online payment platform (per TEB's own campaign page, the only Kosovo bank at the time offering installment payments via "Starcard"), 3D Secure-certified, accepting Visa/Mastercard globally; TEB's own page does not explicitly state a business-registration requirement or name WooCommerce. ## 5. EU VAT on digital goods

Per the European Commission's own "Your Europe" business-taxation portal, the **non-Union OSS scheme** exists specifically for this situation: *"The Non-Union scheme is for businesses established outside the EU, with no EU fixed establishment"*, covering B2C digital/consultancy/professional services sold to EU consumers. Key points, per the same official page:

- **No EU physical presence required** to register — "you don't need to have any physical presence in the EU."
- **Single registration, single return:** *"you can choose any one EU country to register for OSS and submit a quarterly VAT return through that country's OSS portal"* — one registration covers all 27 member states rather than registering in each one separately.
- **Destination-based VAT:** *"you charge VAT at the rate of the customer's country"* — i.e., a sale to a French consumer is charged French VAT, a sale to a German consumer German VAT, etc., all reported and remitted through the single OSS return.
- **No threshold mentioned for non-EU sellers:** the official EU page describes the non-Union scheme without citing any minimum-sales threshold before registration is required (the €10,000/year micro-business threshold that lets *EU-established* sellers delay switching to destination-country VAT is explicitly limited to EU-established businesses, per the same source and corroborating third-party tax-compliance summaries). In practice this is widely reported by VAT-compliance providers as "VAT is due from the first sale" for non-EU sellers — this specific "first sale" framing is corroborated by secondary sources (not itself quoted verbatim on the europa.eu page fetched), so treat the "from the very first euro" framing as strongly-supported-but-not-verbatim-EU-sourced, while the absence of any published threshold **is** directly confirmed on the official page.

Source: [europa.eu — VAT One Stop Shop](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/one-stop-shop/index_en.htm)

**Who absorbs this obligation vs. who doesn't:**

- **Stripe and PayPal do not act as seller/merchant of record and do not handle VAT registration or filing for you.** Stripe's own resource page states that when a business sells directly to customers on its own site (i.e., standard Stripe, not Stripe's separate "Managed Payments" MoR product), *"the business typically acts as both the SoR and the MoR"* — meaning **you** retain "Tax calculation, collection, and remittance" responsibility. ([Stripe's own page](https://stripe.com/resources/more/seller-of-record)) PayPal's User Agreement states materially the same thing in its Taxes section: *"It is your responsibility to determine what, if any, taxes apply to the payments you make or receive, and it is your responsibility to collect, report and remit the correct tax to the appropriate tax authority. PayPal is not responsible for determining whether taxes apply to your transaction, or for collecting, reporting or remitting any taxes arising from any transaction."* (This exact clause was confirmed in an archived version of [PayPal's User Agreement PDF](https://www.paypalobjects.com/ua/US_en_US/ua.pdf), and matches the substance of PayPal's current agreement per corroborating search-indexed excerpts of the live document.)
- **Kosovo bank card-acquiring gateways** (ProCredit, Raiffeisen, TEB, BKT, NLB) are pure payment processors, not sellers of record — none of their own pages mention any VAT/tax handling on the merchant's behalf. VAT compliance is 100% on the merchant.
- **Merchant-of-Record platforms (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Gumroad, FastSpring) are specifically built to absorb this.** Paddle's own pricing page states its all-inclusive fee covers "global tax compliance (Merchant of Record services)" ([source](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)); this is the standard MoR value proposition across all four — they become the legal seller of record for tax purposes and handle VAT/sales-tax calculation, collection and remittance across the markets they support, in exchange for their higher transaction fee.

**Practical implication:** if the merchant uses Stripe (via Atlas), a Kosovo bank gateway, or any non-MoR rail, they must personally register for the non-Union OSS scheme (in any one EU member state of their choosing) and file quarterly VAT returns themselves. If they use an MoR that actually accepts Kosovo as a seller country, VAT is handled for them — but see §4 for how unresolved Kosovo eligibility is for all four MoRs, and §6 for the WooCommerce-integration cost of going the MoR route.

**Summary of the WooCommerce-integration picture:** the *only* rails with a currently-official, actively-maintained, native WooCommerce checkout plugin are **Stripe** (requires forming a US entity via Stripe Atlas, since Kosovo itself is unsupported) and **Raiffeisen Bank Kosovo** (requires a registered Kosovo business). Every Merchant-of-Record option — the category built specifically to solve the "unsupported country + VAT" problem — currently has either no WooCommerce plugin, an official plugin that deliberately doesn't do WooCommerce checkout, or an official plugin its own maker says is abandoned. This is the finding flagged at the top of this document.
